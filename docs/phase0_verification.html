Why the ICC values drift

The notebook's ICC cell samples N_PER_SUBJECT = 3 sessions per subject. Those values will move between runs depending on which sessions get drawn — so the mismatch is probably not an error in either document, it's a statistic being quoted to three decimals when it doesn't have three decimals of stability. Either raise the sample and re-run, or quote them as approximate. Temperature at 0.522 is high enough that the conclusion drawn from it (absolute temperature works as a subject fingerprint) survives either way; the EDA and HR values are small enough that the exact figure carries less weight than the plan's prose implies.

The one that actually blocks something

The SCAR result has no source in the repo. No propensity model, no AUC output, nothing that produces 0.728 or the 5.8× spread. It's marked "derived" in Phase 0, so it came from somewhere — most likely a separate session — but it isn't reproducible from anything committed.

This is the most load-bearing claim in the plan. It is the entire justification for subject-stratified nnPU (§3.3), for rejecting a pooled decision threshold (§3.0), and for figure F06. AUDIT.md section A7 is built to compute exactly this and expects exactly those values. Until it runs, several Phase 3 design decisions are resting on an unverified number — and if the real spread is narrower, some of them get simpler.

What to run first

  1. A10 — the event-triggered average. Align every level-2 event at onset, average EDA and HR from 30 minutes before to 60 minutes after, pooled and then faceted by the six subjects with the most events. Both documents independently flag this as the cheapest possible kill-shot, and the audit says to run it even if earlier sections are incomplete. A flat trace means the labels don't mark a physiologically distinguishable state at this resolution — and no amount of PU learning, negative curation, or pretraining repairs that. A visible rise also hands us the empirical answer to the boundary-trimming question for free.
  2. A1 through A9, in order. Roughly ten scripts writing to reports/audit/ and figures/audit/, read-only against the archive. A7 settles the SCAR question above; A5 settles whether HRV enters the baseline feature set or gets demoted to an ablation; A8 settles the negative ratio before it gets fixed rather than after.
A correction to how we'd been sequencing this

We had been treating self-supervised pretraining as an early move — use the large unlabelled pool to learn a representation, then fine-tune on the scarce labels. The plan's framing is sharper: the two problems are independent. Having no negative class (87.7% of sensor time unlabelled) and having too few positives (178 level-2 events across 14 usable subjects) are separate failures with separate fixes.

Negatives are Phases 2 and 3. Pretraining is Phase 4, and it is optional — gated on Phase 3 clearing a floor worth improving on. The line that settles the ordering: pretraining does not manufacture negatives, and a well-pretrained model fitted against a biased target is still biased.
